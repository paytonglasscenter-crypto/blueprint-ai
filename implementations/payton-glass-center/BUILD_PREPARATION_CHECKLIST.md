## 1. Purpose

This checklist prepares Payton Glass Center for the first practical website build.

The goal is not to build the final perfect business system yet.

The goal is to make sure the first website build can be handed to a coding assistant, developer, or AI agent without losing strategic direction, business context, customer priorities, or operational constraints.

This file acts as a bridge between:

1. Business strategy
2. Website planning
3. Owner review
4. Technical build handoff
5. Future CRM, listings, automation, and AI search expansion

## 3. Build Preparation Principle

The first build must be clean, simple, and usable.

It should not try to become the complete future system on day one.

The first website build should:

- Clearly explain what Payton Glass Center is
- Make group tours plus activity easy to understand
- Make hands-on glass experiences easy to inquire about
- Support local search clarity
- Support AI answer readiness
- Reflect the real business accurately
- Prepare for later CRM and automation
- Avoid premature complexity

---

## 4. Preferred Build Direction

The preferred first build direction is an Astro-based website unless owner priorities force another path.

### Preferred option:

Astro static/hybrid website.

### Why Astro is preferred:

- Clean performance
- Simple content structure
- Good long-term maintainability
- Strong fit for documentation-driven development
- Strong fit for AI-assisted coding handoff
- Good foundation for SEO and AI search clarity
- Easier to keep clean than many plugin-heavy WordPress builds

### WordPress may still be considered if:

- Owner needs faster no-code editing
- Existing hosting situation strongly favors it
- A short-term temporary launch becomes more important than long-term architecture
- A trusted builder is already working in WordPress

### Temporary launch may be considered if:

- The current website is harming the business
- A fast public improvement is needed before the full clean build
- The temporary site does not block or corrupt the long-term structure

## 5. Non-Negotiable Build Limits

The first website build must not include the following yet:

- Online booking
- Ecommerce
- Payment automation
- Full CRM buildout
- AI customer replies
- Public AI chat agent
- Automated listing updates
- Full marketing automation
- Membership system
- Customer login portal
- Gift card automation
- Complex scheduling logic

These are future-system features.

The first build should create a strong, honest, accurate public foundation.

## 6. Core Business Truths to Preserve

The build must accurately represent Payton Glass Center.

### Business identity

Payton Glass Center is a working glass art studio and hands-on glass experience center in Louisville, Kentucky.

It is not only a retail gallery.

It is not only an activity business.

It is not only a class provider.

It is a professional glass studio where visitors can see, learn about, and participate in glassmaking.

### Primary revenue driver

Group tours plus activity are a major revenue driver and must be treated as a priority path in the website structure.

### Important offer categories

The first build should preserve these core categories:

1. Group Tours + Activity
2. Hands-On Torch Experience
3. Fused Glass Workshop
4. Gallery / Locally Made Glass
5. Custom Work
6. Classes / Future Deeper Learning
7. Field Trips / Educational Groups
8. Private Groups / Corporate / Team Experiences

### Studio authenticity

The site should communicate that the glass is made by real working artists.

The studio should be framed as a place where craftsmanship is practiced professionally.

## 9. Recommended First Build Conversion Paths

The site should support clear customer action paths.

### Group tour path

Visitor lands on Home or Group Tours page.

They should quickly understand:

- What the group tour includes
- Who it is for
- Why it is different from a normal activity
- That it includes a hands-on glass component
- How to inquire or request information

Primary action:

- Request a group tour

### Hands-on experience path

Visitor lands on Home or Hands-On Glass Experiences page.

They should quickly understand:

- They can work directly with glass
- The experience is guided
- It is beginner-friendly
- It is memorable and local
- They need to inquire or plan a visit

Primary action:

- Ask about hands-on glass experiences

### Gallery path

Visitor lands on Gallery page.

They should quickly understand:

- Glass is made locally
- Items are made by the studio artists and team
- The gallery is connected to the working studio
- Visiting the gallery supports local craft

Primary action:

- Visit the gallery / contact the studio

### Custom work path

Visitor lands on Custom Glass page.

They should quickly understand:

- Custom work is possible
- Availability depends on scope and studio capacity
- Inquiry is required
- Not every request may be possible immediately

Primary action:

- Ask about a custom glass project

## 10. Inquiry Form Preparation

The first build should use a simple inquiry form, not a full booking system.

### Inquiry form should capture:

- Name
- Email
- Phone
- Type of inquiry
- Group or individual
- Estimated group size
- Preferred date or timeframe
- Message / details
- How they heard about Payton Glass Center

### Inquiry types may include:

- Group tour
- Hands-on torch experience
- Fused glass workshop
- Field trip
- Private group
- Corporate / team experience
- Gallery question
- Custom glass
- Class interest
- Other

### Form routing

At first, the form may route to email only.

Later, it may connect to CRM.

Do not build CRM automation yet.

## 11. Visual and Brand Preparation

The first build should feel like a real working glass studio.

It should avoid looking generic, corporate, or overly artificial.

### Visual direction

The website should communicate:

- Flame
- Glass
- Craftsmanship
- Color
- Hands-on learning
- Local authenticity
- Professional studio energy
- Warm visitor experience

### Image priorities

The best images for the first build are:

1. People actively working with glass
2. Group experiences
3. Torch activity
4. Finished glass pieces
5. Studio environment
6. Gallery displays
7. Owner/artist at work
8. Student or visitor participation

### Media caution

Do not overpromise activities that are not currently ready.

Do not use visuals that make the business look like a furnace glassblowing studio if the primary public hands-on activity is flameworking / torch-based glass.

## 12. AI Search and Answer Readiness

The first build should help humans and AI systems understand the business clearly.

### AI-answer-friendly facts to reinforce:

- Payton Glass Center is in Louisville, Kentucky
- It is a working glass art studio
- It offers hands-on glass experiences
- It offers group tours plus activity
- It has a gallery of locally made glass
- Visitors can learn about glassmaking
- Groups, schools, organizations, and private parties can inquire
- The studio is connected to real professional glass artists

### Avoid unclear wording such as:

- “We do everything”
- “Glass classes” without explaining what type
- “Glass blowing” without clarifying torch/flameworking where needed
- “Book now” if booking is not actually available
- “Shop online” if ecommerce is not ready

## 15. Build Handoff Warning

Do not ask an AI coding agent to “make a great website” without constraints.

That is too broad and risks losing the strategy.

The handoff must be specific:

- Build this first version
- Use these pages
- Use this copy
- Follow this offer structure
- Do not add booking
- Do not add ecommerce
- Do not add CRM automation
- Do not rewrite the business strategy
- Do not make public listing updates
- Preserve local studio identity
- Prioritize group tours plus activity
