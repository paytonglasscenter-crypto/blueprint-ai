## 1. Purpose

This document defines the Group Tours + Activity system for Payton Glass Center.

Group tours are a major revenue driver and should be treated as a repeatable business system, not just a casual offer.

The purpose of this document is to clarify:

- Who group tours are for
- What customers need to understand
- How inquiries should be handled
- What information must be collected
- What the tour experience should include
- What website content is needed
- What local search profiles should communicate
- What CRM fields will eventually be needed
- What automations should eventually support the process

## 2. Strategic Importance

Group tours plus activity are one of the strongest current revenue opportunities for Payton Glass Center.

They combine several strengths of the business:

- A real working glass art studio
- Professional artists
- Educational storytelling
- Hands-on creative activity
- Group-friendly experience
- Local uniqueness
- Repeatable booking opportunity
- Strong review and referral potential

Group tours should be treated as one of the primary pillars of the business.

## 3. Core Group Tour Description

A Payton Glass Center group tour gives visitors access to a professional working glass art studio where they can learn about glassmaking, see the creative process, interact with artists, and participate in a hands-on glass activity.

The experience should communicate that Payton Glass Center is not only an activity venue. It is a real studio where artists make their living through skilled craft.

## 4. Primary Customer Segments

Group tours may serve:

### Schools

Schools may want educational value, safety, age-appropriate activities, clear scheduling, and a memorable field trip.

### Homeschool Groups

Homeschool groups may want flexible educational activities, mixed-age suitability, and hands-on learning.

### Scouts and Youth Groups

Youth groups may want creative activities, badges or enrichment value, safety guidance, and structured group participation.

### Senior Groups

Senior groups may want accessible, interesting, social, creative experiences with clear timing and comfort expectations.

### Corporate Teams

Corporate teams may want team-building value, a unique shared activity, and simple coordination.

### Church Groups

Church groups may want group-friendly creative outings, fellowship activities, and clear pricing.

### Tourist Groups

Tourist groups may want a unique Louisville experience that is easy to understand and schedule.

### Birthday and Private Groups

Private groups may want something different from typical party venues, with clear activity options and pricing.

### Community Organizations

Community organizations may want educational, cultural, or creative programming.

## 7. Educational Storytelling

Group tours should include a clear educational story.

Important messages:

- Glassmaking is a skilled craft.
- Flameworking is glassmaking at the torch.
- This is different from furnace glassblowing.
- Glass artists can make a real living through the craft.
- Payton Glass Center and connected studios are professional glass art studios.
- Creative skill can become a livelihood.
- Visitors are entering a real working studio, not a staged attraction.

This is especially valuable for children and students because it shows that art, craft, skill, entrepreneurship, and livelihood can connect.

The activity should be:

Activity details to define later:

- Age suitability
- Time required

- Whether items go home same day

The group booking path should eventually follow this structure:

2. Customer reads basic details
3. Customer submits inquiry or calls
4. Business collects group details
5. Business confirms availability
6. Business sends price and expectations
7. Customer confirms date
8. Deposit or payment is collected if applicable
9. Confirmation is sent
10. Pre-visit instructions are sent
11. Group attends
12. Pickup or completion instructions are handled

## 15. Tour Flow Draft

A standard tour flow may look like:

1. Welcome group
2. Confirm group leader and participant count
3. Introduce Payton Glass Center
4. Explain that this is a professional working glass studio
5. Explain flameworking and how it differs from furnace glassblowing
6. Give safety overview
7. Demonstrate glassmaking
8. Explain the hands-on activity
9. Begin activity rotation or group activity
10. Answer questions during the process
11. Discuss glass as art, craft, career, and business
12. Explain cooling, firing, pickup, or take-home process
13. Invite group to look around or purchase glass art if appropriate
14. Thank group
15. Encourage reviews, referrals, photos, or future visits

---

## 16. After-Visit Process

After the group visit, the business should track:

- Whether the group was satisfied
