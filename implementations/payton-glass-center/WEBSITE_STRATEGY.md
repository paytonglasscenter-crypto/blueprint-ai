## 1. Purpose

This document defines the website strategy for Payton Glass Center based on the business blueprint.

The website should not be treated as only a brochure, gallery, or design project.

The website should become the public operating layer of the business.

It should help customers understand the business, choose the right offer, inquire or book, prepare for their visit, and return after the experience.

The website should support:

- Customer clarity
- Group tour growth
- Hands-on activity bookings
- Local search visibility
- AI search readiness
- Google Business Profile alignment
- Bing Places alignment
- Apple Business Connect alignment
- CRM preparation
- Future automation
- Social media content conversion
- Long-term business growth

---

## 2. Strategic Website Role

The website has five major jobs:

1. Explain what Payton Glass Center is.
2. Make the main offers easy to understand.
3. Convert visitors into inquiries, bookings, calls, visits, and reviews.
4. Act as the source of truth for local search and AI search.
5. Prepare the business for CRM and automation.

The website should be built from the business model outward, not from a visual theme inward.

---

## 3. Core Website Message

The core website message should be:

Payton Glass Center is a working glass art studio in Louisville, Kentucky where guests can watch, learn, and create through hands-on glass experiences, group tours, fused glass workshops, and artist-led activities.

Supporting message:

Real artists. Real glassmaking. Hands-on creative experiences.

---

## 4. Primary Website Audience

The website should serve several audiences, but not all equally.

### Priority 1 — Group Organizers

This includes schools, homeschool groups, scouts, senior groups, corporate teams, church groups, tourist groups, birthday groups, and community organizations.

They need clarity, confidence, logistics, pricing guidance, and a simple inquiry path.

### Priority 2 — Families and Local Activity Seekers

They need to quickly understand what they can do, whether children can participate, how much it costs, and whether they need to book ahead.

### Priority 3 — Tourists

They need to know whether the studio is worth adding to a Louisville visit, where it is located, what the experience includes, and how to schedule.

### Priority 4 — Creative Adults and Class Seekers

They need to understand basic experiences, deeper learning options, future classes, and how to join an interest list.

### Priority 5 — Gift Buyers and Custom Glass Customers

They need to understand handmade glass art, custom requests, gift options, and how to contact the studio.

## 10. Search and AI Readiness Strategy

The website should be written so both humans and AI systems can understand it.

Content should use:

- Clear page titles
- Descriptive headings
- Direct answers
- Plain-language explanations
- Consistent offer names
- Louisville location references
- FAQ sections
- Internal links between related pages
- Descriptive photo alt text later
- Structured data/schema later
- Customer-friendly terms and accurate studio terms together

Example language strategy:

Many people search for glassblowing, but Payton Glass Center specializes in flameworking, a form of glassmaking done at the torch instead of from a furnace.

This keeps the site findable while teaching the correct terminology.

## 13. Content Tone

The tone should be:

- Clear
- Welcoming
- Confident
- Educational
- Professional
- Hands-on
- Artist-led
- Local
- Practical

Avoid:

- Vague art language
- Overly fancy wording
- Confusing offer names
- Too much technical glass terminology without explanation
- Making the business sound like only a store
- Making the business sound like only a classroom
- Making the business sound like only a tourist attraction

The website should feel like a real working studio that knows how to welcome the public.

## 17. Booking Strategy

Not every offer needs online booking immediately.

Recommended early approach:

### Inquiry-Based First

Use inquiry forms for:

- Group tours
- Field trips
- Private events
- Corporate teams
- School groups
- Custom glass
- Advanced classes

### Booking or Scheduled Sessions Later

Potential online booking later for:

- Fused glass workshops
- Small hands-on torch experiences
- Scheduled beginner classes
- Seasonal workshops
- Public events

The business should avoid forcing complex group bookings into a weak online booking flow too early.

## 22. Website Risks

Risks to avoid:

- Starting with visual design before business clarity
- Making group tours hard to find
- Treating the site like only an art gallery
- Hiding the fact that people can participate
- Not explaining flameworking clearly
- Using different offer names than GBP
- Not having dedicated pages for major services
- Using one generic contact form with no structure
- Not answering common customer questions
- Not preparing for CRM
- Not preparing for AI search
- Not showing enough real experience photos
- Not making calls to action obvious
