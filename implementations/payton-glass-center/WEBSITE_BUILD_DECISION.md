## 1. Purpose

This document compares possible website build paths for Payton Glass Center and recommends the best first build approach.

The goal is not to pick the fanciest platform.

The goal is to choose the website path that best supports:

- Fast launch
- Clear business explanation
- Group tour inquiries
- Local search visibility
- AI search readiness
- Simple owner updates
- Structured inquiry capture
- Future CRM connection
- Future automation
- Long-term Blueprint AI alignment

---

## 2. Core Principle

Choose the simplest platform that can support the real business system.

The website is not just a digital brochure.

It is the public operating layer for Payton Glass Center.

But the first version should still stay focused.

Do not overbuild.

Do not choose a platform only because it is trendy.

Do not choose a platform that Mark cannot maintain or that slows the project down.

## 5. Build Path Options

Possible build paths:

1. Website builder
2. WordPress
3. Static site
4. Astro
5. Astro/Starlight-style documentation structure
6. Custom React / Next.js
7. Current website refresh only
8. Hybrid temporary landing site plus later full build

Each option should be judged by business fit, not technical excitement.

---

## 6. Option 1: Website Builder

Examples:

- Wix
- Squarespace
- Webflow
- Hostinger Website Builder
- Similar no-code builder

### Strengths

- Fast to launch
- Visual editing
- Lower technical barrier
- Built-in hosting
- Built-in forms
- Templates available
- Easy enough for basic updates
- Good for simple small business sites

### Weaknesses

- Can become limiting later
- Less control over content structure
- Less flexible for advanced systems
- CRM/automation integration may be limited
- Schema and technical SEO control may vary
- May not align well with long-term Blueprint AI system goals

### Fit for Payton Glass Center

Good for speed.

May be acceptable if the priority is launching a cleaner site quickly.

Risk is that the site becomes another brochure instead of a structured business operating layer.

### Recommendation

Possible short-term option, but not the strongest long-term Blueprint AI fit.

---

## 7. Option 2: WordPress

### Strengths

- Flexible
- Familiar to many website editors
- Strong SEO ecosystem
- Many form plugins
- Many local business features
- Easy to extend
- Good for service business websites
- Can support blog/content later
- Can support schema, forms, galleries, and landing pages

### Weaknesses

- Requires maintenance
- Plugin overload risk
- Security updates
- Hosting quality matters
- Can become slow if poorly built
- Can get messy without discipline
- More moving parts than a static site

### Fit for Payton Glass Center

Strong practical fit if Mark wants an editable local business website with service pages, forms, images, and future content.

WordPress can work well if the build is kept simple and disciplined.

### Recommendation

Strong candidate if ease of editing and common local business functionality matter most.

---

## 8. Option 3: Static Site

Examples:

- Plain HTML/CSS
- Eleventy
- Astro static output
- Other static generators

### Strengths

- Fast
- Secure
- Low maintenance
- Great for SEO if structured well
- Good for content clarity
- Easy to host
- Good for simple service pages
- No plugin bloat
- Strong long-term stability

### Weaknesses

- Editing may require developer workflow
- Forms need external service or backend
- Non-technical updates can be harder
- Media updates need workflow
- Less familiar to typical business owners

### Fit for Payton Glass Center

Strong technical fit for a fast, structured, search-friendly website.

Potential issue is whether Mark wants to edit pages directly or is comfortable managing changes through files/Git/AI-assisted workflow.

### Recommendation

Strong candidate if Blueprint AI will help maintain and evolve the site.

---

## 9. Option 4: Astro

### Strengths

- Excellent for fast content websites
- Strong static site output
- Good markdown/content structure
- Flexible components
- Good SEO control
- Good fit with GitHub workflow
- Aligns with existing Project Echo/Starlight experience
- Can become a structured business knowledge-to-website pipeline
- Supports future AI-assisted content workflows

### Weaknesses

- More technical than WordPress or website builders
- Requires development workflow
- Forms need integration
- Owner editing may require Git, CMS, or AI-assisted process
- Requires platform/hosting setup

### Fit for Payton Glass Center

Very strong fit if the goal is to build a structured, AI-ready, content-driven website connected to the Blueprint AI methodology.

Astro can turn the documents and content model into a fast, organized public website.

### Recommendation

Best strategic fit if Mark is willing to use an AI-assisted development workflow rather than a traditional visual editor.

---

## 10. Option 5: Astro / Starlight-Style Structure

### Description

This would use the same thinking behind documentation sites, but adapted for a public business website.

The site would not look like a manual.

It would use structured content principles:

- Pages are markdown/content-driven
- Offers have clear page models
- FAQs are structured
- Local search content is consistent
- AI-answer-ready content is easier to maintain
- Future documentation and website strategy stay connected

### Strengths

- Very aligned with Blueprint AI
- Strong content structure
- Strong for AEO/GEO/AI search
- Easy to version with Git
- Good for future business documentation integration
- Can support reusable templates for other businesses later

### Weaknesses

- Needs careful design so it does not look like documentation
- Requires technical setup
- Owner editing needs workflow
- More complex than a basic website builder

### Fit for Payton Glass Center

Excellent fit for Blueprint AI as a case study.

This path could become a reusable model for future businesses.

### Recommendation

Strategically excellent, but should be implemented carefully as a customer-facing business website, not a raw documentation site.

---

## 11. Option 6: Custom React / Next.js

### Strengths

- Highly flexible
- Modern development stack
- Can support complex features
- Good for future apps, CRM, booking, dashboards, and integrations
- Full control over user experience

### Weaknesses

- Overkill for first build
- More expensive/time-consuming
- More development complexity
- More maintenance
- Not needed for first website version
- Could delay launch

### Fit for Payton Glass Center

Not recommended for the first website build.

This may become useful later if Payton Glass Center grows into custom booking, CRM, customer portal, ecommerce, or AI-agent-enabled workflows.

### Recommendation

Do not build yet.

---

## 12. Option 7: Current Website Refresh Only

### Strengths

- Fastest possible path if current access exists
- Least disruptive
- Can fix obvious problems quickly
- May preserve existing search signals
- Lower effort

### Weaknesses

- May inherit old structure problems
- May not support proper inquiry flow
- May not support modern local/AEO/GEO strategy
- Could patch the outdated site instead of solving the problem
- May not create a scalable Blueprint AI case study

### Fit for Payton Glass Center

Useful only if the current site platform is workable and can be improved quickly.

Not ideal if the current site structure is outdated, hard to edit, or not aligned with the new business model.

### Recommendation

Consider only as a temporary emergency improvement.

---

## 13. Option 8: Hybrid Temporary Landing Site Plus Later Full Build

### Description

Create a simple new public foundation quickly while continuing to build the full system later.

### Strengths

- Faster than full build
- Can focus on group tours and inquiries
- Can improve public presence quickly
- Reduces risk of overbuilding
- Lets owner data collection continue
- Can test messaging before full build

### Weaknesses

- May create duplicate work
- May require migration later
- Might feel incomplete
- Needs careful domain/SEO handling

### Fit for Payton Glass Center

Good if speed is urgent and the current site is hurting the business.

Could launch a focused first version with:

- Home
- Group Tours
- Contact
- FAQ
- Visit

Then expand.

### Recommendation

Strong practical option if speed is the highest priority.

## 15. Recommended Direction

Recommended first strategic direction:

### Astro-based structured static website

This is the best Blueprint AI-aligned path if the project is being built with AI-assisted development and GitHub-backed documentation.

Why:

- Fast
- Clean
- Search-friendly
- Markdown/content-friendly
- Works with GitHub
- Good for structured pages
- Good for local SEO
- Good for AEO/GEO/AI search preparation
- Can reuse patterns from Project Echo
- Can become a repeatable model for future Blueprint AI implementations

### Practical caution

The site must look and feel like a real public business website.

It should not look like a documentation site.

The Starlight/documentation concept is useful for internal structure, not necessarily for the public visual design.

---

## 16. Secondary Recommendation

If speed and owner editing are more important than Blueprint AI structure, use:

### WordPress with disciplined page architecture

This is the best practical small-business fallback.

WordPress is a good choice if:

- Mark wants easier editing
- The site needs to be handed off to non-technical people
- Existing hosting supports WordPress
- A quick local business site is the priority
- Plugins are kept minimal
- The build is carefully managed

---

## 17. Not Recommended for First Build

Do not use these for the first build:

### Custom React / Next.js

Too much complexity for the first version.

### Full ecommerce platform

Not enough product/fulfillment detail yet.

### Full booking platform

Pricing, capacity, schedule, and approval rules are not confirmed.

### Heavy CRM-first build

Inquiry workflow needs to be tested first.

---

## 18. Recommended First Build Path

Recommended first build path:

1. Complete owner data for critical launch facts.
2. Decide Astro vs WordPress.
3. Build website foundation pages.
4. Add structured inquiry form.
5. Add core local SEO metadata.
6. Add FAQ sections.
7. Add selected media.
8. Set up analytics/search tools.
9. Launch first version.
10. Align Google Business Profile, Bing Places, Apple Business Connect, and major listings.
11. Track inquiries.
12. Decide CRM first step after real inquiry flow is observed.
13. Add safe automations later.

## 20. Decision Needed From Mark

Mark needs to decide which priority matters most right now.

### Option A: Fastest Useful Launch

Choose a focused landing-style website or current-site refresh.

Best when:

- Current site is hurting the business
- Speed matters most
- Full structure can come later

### Option B: Best Long-Term Blueprint AI Fit

Choose Astro-based structured website.

Best when:

- The site should become a repeatable business architecture model
- GitHub/AI-assisted workflow is acceptable
- Search/AEO/GEO structure matters
- Future systems matter

### Option C: Practical Editable Small-Business Site

Choose WordPress.

Best when:

- Easy editing matters most
- Familiar local business tools matter
- The site may need non-technical maintenance

---

## 21. Provisional Recommendation

For Payton Glass Center and Blueprint AI, the provisional recommendation is:

### Build an Astro-based structured static website for the first serious rebuild.

Reason:

It best supports the long-term goal of turning Payton Glass Center into the first complete Blueprint AI implementation and case study.

However, if Mark needs a public improvement extremely quickly, a temporary focused launch may come first.
