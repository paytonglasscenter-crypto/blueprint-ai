## 1. Purpose

This document summarizes Implementation Phase 1 for Payton Glass Center inside the Blueprint AI repository.

The goal of this phase was to stop adding abstract framework documents and begin applying Blueprint AI to a real business in depth.

Payton Glass Center is the first implementation and validation case study for Blueprint AI.

This phase proves that Blueprint AI can move from philosophy and framework into practical business documentation, website strategy, local search alignment, CRM planning, and automation readiness.

## 2. Phase 1 Strategic Direction

Blueprint AI is no longer being treated as a website project.

Blueprint AI is a universal methodology for designing AI-powered businesses.

Payton Glass Center is the first implementation used to validate the methodology.

The purpose of Implementation Phase 1 was to document the business clearly before building:

- Website pages
- Local search updates
- Google Business Profile changes
- Bing Places changes
- Apple Business Connect changes
- CRM systems
- Automation workflows
- Booking systems
- AI agent workflows
- Marketing campaigns

The guiding principle was:

Do not automate confusion.

The business must be understood before systems are built around it.

## 4. Business Intake Summary

The business intake clarified that Payton Glass Center is:

A working glass art studio in Louisville, Kentucky where guests can watch, learn, and create through hands-on glass experiences, group tours, fused glass workshops, torch-based glassmaking activities, educational visits, private group events, custom glass work, handmade glass art, and future class opportunities.

Core identity:

- Working glass art studio
- Hands-on glass experience center
- Professional artist-led studio
- Local Louisville creative destination
- Educational and experiential business

Important business truth:

Group tours plus activity are a major revenue driver and should be treated as a core business system.

## 5. Customer Journey Summary

The customer journey was mapped from discovery through repeat relationship.

Core journey stages:

1. Discovery
2. Understanding
3. Interest
4. Decision
5. Inquiry or booking
6. Pre-visit preparation
7. Arrival
8. Tour or activity experience
9. Completion and pickup
10. Follow-up
11. Review, referral, repeat visit, or deeper class

The journey showed that customers need clarity before they can book.

Common customer confusion includes:

- Whether the studio is open to the public
- Whether guests can make something
- Difference between flameworking and furnace glassblowing
- Whether activities are for kids, adults, families, or groups
- Whether projects go home same day
- Whether reservations are required
- How group tours work

This customer journey should shape the website, listings, CRM, and automation.

## 9. Website Strategy Summary

The website strategy defined the website as the public operating layer of the business.

The website has five major jobs:

1. Explain what Payton Glass Center is.
2. Make the main offers easy to understand.
3. Convert visitors into inquiries, bookings, calls, visits, and reviews.
4. Act as the source of truth for local search and AI search.
5. Prepare the business for CRM and automation.

Primary website conversion goal:

Group tour and activity inquiries.

Core website message:

Payton Glass Center is a working glass art studio in Louisville, Kentucky where guests can watch, learn, and create through hands-on glass experiences, group tours, fused glass workshops, and artist-led activities.

Supporting message:

Real artists. Real glassmaking. Hands-on creative experiences.

## 16. Phase 1 Key Decisions

Key decisions made during this phase:

- Blueprint AI is a business methodology, not a website project.
- Payton Glass Center is the first validation implementation.
- Group tours plus activity are a major revenue driver.
- Website strategy must come from business strategy.
- Local search, AI search, and the website must align.
- Google Business Profile, Bing Places, and Apple Business Connect matter.
- The website should become the public operating layer of the business.
- CRM should not be selected before field needs are documented.
- Automation should not be built before the business workflow is documented.
- Future advanced classes are a growth opportunity.
- The business should be positioned as a professional working glass studio and hands-on experience center.
