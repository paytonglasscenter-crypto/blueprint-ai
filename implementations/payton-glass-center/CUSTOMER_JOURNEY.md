# Payton Glass Center Customer Journey

Status: Draft
Blueprint AI Phase: Implementation Phase 1.2
Business: Payton Glass Center
Location: Louisville, Kentucky
Depends On: BUSINESS_INTAKE.md

---

## 1. Purpose

This document maps how customers discover, understand, evaluate, book, experience, and return to Payton Glass Center.

The goal is to make the customer path clear before building the website, search profiles, CRM, automations, booking flows, or follow-up systems.

A strong customer journey helps answer:

- What does the customer need to understand?
- What questions do they ask?
- What trust signals do they need?
- What blocks them from booking?
- What should the website make obvious?
- What should local search listings explain?
- What should the CRM track?
- What automations will eventually help?

---

## 2. Core Customer Journey Summary

The basic customer journey is:

1. Discovery
2. Understanding
3. Interest
4. Decision
5. Inquiry or booking
6. Pre-visit preparation
7. Arrival
8. Tour or activity experience
9. Completion and pickup
10. Follow-up
11. Review, referral, repeat visit, or deeper class

## 4. Journey Stage 2 — Understanding

### What Customers Need to Understand

Customers must understand the difference between:

- Watching a demonstration
- Taking a tour
- Participating in a fused glass activity
- Working directly at the torch
- Booking a group tour
- Signing up for a class
- Buying glass art
- Requesting custom work

### Common Confusion Points

Customers may not know:

- The difference between furnace glassblowing and flameworking.
- Whether they personally make the item or assist with it.
- Whether children can participate.
- Whether a project is finished the same day.
- Whether glass needs to be cooled, fired, or picked up later.
- Whether walk-ins are accepted.
- Whether groups require advance scheduling.
- How long activities take.
- What size groups can be accommodated.

### Business Goal at This Stage

Turn curiosity into clarity.

The website, listings, phone scripts, and AI search answers should explain the business in plain language.

Customers may contact Payton Glass Center for:

- Group tours
- School field trips
- Birthday activities
- Fused glass workshops
- Torch experiences
- Private events
- Custom glass

- General visit questions

For inquiries, the business should capture:

- Phone number
- Email
- Preferred date
- Preferred time
- Group type
- Group size
- Ages of participants
- Desired activity

---

## 8. Journey Stage 6 — Pre-Visit Preparation

### What Customers Need Before Arrival

Customers may need to know:

- Address and parking information
- Arrival time
- Length of visit
- Clothing or safety expectations
- Whether food or drinks are allowed
- Whether glass items are ready same day
- Payment expectations
- Group rules
- Chaperone requirements for youth groups
- Accessibility information
- Cancellation or rescheduling policy

### Business Goal at This Stage

Reduce confusion before the customer arrives.

A clear confirmation message or pre-visit email can prevent repeated phone calls and improve the experience.

---

## 9. Journey Stage 7 — Arrival

### Customer Experience on Arrival

When customers arrive, they should quickly understand:

- Where to enter
- Who to talk to
- Where the group should gather
- What activity they are doing
- What safety expectations apply
- What happens first

### Business Goal at This Stage

Create confidence and orientation.

The first few minutes should make customers feel welcomed, safe, and excited.

---

## 10. Journey Stage 8 — Tour or Activity Experience

### Experience Components

A strong Payton Glass Center experience may include:

- Welcome and orientation
- Studio introduction
- Explanation of glassmaking
- Safety overview
- Artist demonstration
- Hands-on activity
- Storytelling about the working studio
- Explanation of how artists make a living through glass
- Project completion instructions
- Questions and answers
- Retail or gallery opportunity

### Important Message During the Experience

Payton Glass Center should clearly communicate:

This is a real professional glass art studio where artists make their living through the craft.

This message is especially valuable for children, students, and families because it shows that skilled creative work can be a real career and livelihood.

### Business Goal at This Stage

Deliver an experience that feels educational, personal, memorable, and worth recommending.

---

## 11. Journey Stage 9 — Completion and Pickup

### Customer Needs After the Activity

Depending on the activity, customers may need to know:

- Whether they take the item home same day
- Whether the item must cool
- Whether the item must be kiln fired
- When pickup is available
- Whether shipping is available
- How items are labeled
- What happens if they forget to pick up an item

### Business Goal at This Stage

Make completion and pickup clear, simple, and reliable.

This stage may later require process documentation and automation.

## 14. Customer Journey by Segment

### Families

Families need clear activity descriptions, age guidance, safety reassurance, pricing, and scheduling information.

### Tourists

Tourists need fast clarity, location information, availability, uniqueness, and proof that the experience is worth adding to their trip.

### Schools and Educational Groups

Schools need educational value, safety information, group capacity, pricing, timing, chaperone expectations, and planning details.

### Homeschool Groups

Homeschool groups need flexible scheduling, educational framing, age range clarity, and hands-on learning value.

### Corporate Groups

Corporate groups need team-building value, group size capacity, scheduling, pricing, parking, and ease of coordination.

### Birthday and Private Groups

Private groups need activity options, pricing, timing, age limits, reservation requirements, and what is included.

### Creative Adults

Creative adults need class options, skill level expectations, project examples, deeper learning pathways, and future class opportunities.
