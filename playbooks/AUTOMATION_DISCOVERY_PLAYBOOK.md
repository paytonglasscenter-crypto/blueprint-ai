# Blueprint AI Genesis 0.7 — Automation Discovery Playbook

## Purpose

This playbook helps identify which parts of a business are good candidates for automation and which parts should remain human-led.

## Step 1 — List Repeating Work

Identify tasks that happen repeatedly.

Examples:

- Answering common questions
- Booking customers
- Sending reminders
- Posting content
- Updating business listings
- Following up after visits
- Requesting reviews
- Organizing leads
- Preparing quotes
- Creating class or event descriptions

## Step 2 — Classify the Task

For each task, classify it as:

- Customer communication
- Internal operation
- Marketing
- Sales
- Fulfillment
- Reporting
- Search visibility
- Knowledge management
- Financial or administrative

## Step 3 — Score the Task

Rate each task from 1 to 5.

### Frequency

How often does it happen?

1 = rarely  
5 = daily or constantly

### Pain

How annoying, costly, or time-consuming is it?

1 = low pain  
5 = high pain

### Clarity

How well is the process understood?

1 = unclear  
5 = very clear

### Risk

How harmful would a mistake be?

1 = low risk  
5 = high risk

## Step 4 — Choose the Automation Level

Use this rule:

- High frequency + high clarity + low risk = good automation candidate
- High frequency + low clarity = document first
- High risk = human approval required
- Low frequency + high judgment = usually not worth automating yet

## Step 5 — Define the Human Approval Point

For each automation candidate, decide:

- Does a human approve before action?
- Does a human review after action?
- Does the system act automatically only inside safe rules?
- Does the system only prepare work but not execute?

## Step 6 — Build the First Automation Backlog

Create a short list of practical automation opportunities.

Recommended columns:

- Task
- Business area
- Current process
- Pain level
- Risk level
- Automation level
- Owner
- Tool or system
- First version
- Notes

## Payton Glass Center First Candidates

Possible early automation candidates:

- Group tour inquiry intake
- Customer FAQ drafts
- Follow-up emails after workshops
- Review request reminders
- Google Business Profile content planning
- Social media idea generation
- Offer descriptions
- Local search listing consistency checks
- Class interest list tracking
- Website FAQ expansion

## Principle

The best first automations are not the flashiest. They are the ones that remove repeated friction without creating confusion.
