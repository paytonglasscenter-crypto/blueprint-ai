# Blueprint AI Genesis 0.7 — CRM Foundation Playbook

## Purpose

This playbook defines how Blueprint AI approaches CRM design.

A CRM is not just contact storage. A CRM is the memory system for customer relationships, opportunities, communication, booking behavior, follow-up, and long-term growth.

## CRM Purpose

A Blueprint AI CRM should help a business:

- Remember customers
- Understand customer needs
- Track inquiries and opportunities
- Follow up consistently
- Segment audiences
- Support marketing
- Support repeat visits
- Support reviews and referrals
- Prepare data for AI assistance

## Core CRM Entities

At minimum, the CRM should understand:

### Contacts

People who interact with the business.

Examples:

- Individual customers
- Parents
- Teachers
- Event planners
- Tour organizers
- Corporate contacts
- Artists
- Vendors

### Organizations

Groups connected to contacts.

Examples:

- Schools
- Companies
- Clubs
- Churches
- Tourist groups
- Nonprofits
- Senior groups
- Homeschool groups

### Opportunities

Potential revenue or engagement.

Examples:

- Group tour inquiry
- Birthday party interest
- Workshop booking
- Custom glass inquiry
- Class registration interest
- Corporate team building request

### Activities

Interactions that happened.

Examples:

- Phone call
- Email
- Form submission
- Visit
- Tour
- Workshop
- Quote sent
- Follow-up needed
- Review requested

### Offers

The thing a customer may buy or book.

Examples:

- Group tour plus activity
- Fused glass workshop
- Torch activity
- Custom glass order
- Class
- Event
- Gift certificate

## CRM Design Rule

The CRM should match the business model, not force the business into generic software categories.

## Payton Glass Center CRM Priorities

Early CRM priorities may include:

- Group tour leads
- School and organization contacts
- Birthday and private event inquiries
- Workshop participants
- Repeat customers
- Review follow-ups
- Class interest lists
- Newsletter or content audience
- Custom glass inquiries

## Minimum Useful CRM

The first version should answer:

- Who contacted us?
- What are they interested in?
- What offer does it connect to?
- What stage are they in?
- What should happen next?
- Who is responsible?
- When should we follow up?
- What source did they come from?

## Stages Example

For group tours:

1. New inquiry
2. Needs response
3. Details requested
4. Quote or availability sent
5. Tentatively scheduled
6. Confirmed
7. Completed
8. Follow-up sent
9. Review requested
10. Repeat opportunity

## AI Readiness

A CRM becomes AI-ready when data is structured, consistent, and useful.

AI should be able to assist with:

- Drafting replies
- Summarizing customer history
- Suggesting next steps
- Segmenting leads
- Creating follow-up reminders
- Identifying missed opportunities
- Preparing reports

## Principle

A CRM should help a business treat people better, not merely track them.
