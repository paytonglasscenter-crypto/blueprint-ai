# First Implementation Playbook

**Version:** Genesis 0.3  
**Status:** Foundational Playbook  
**Purpose:** Define the first repeatable process for applying Blueprint AI to a real business.

---

## 1. Purpose

The first implementation proves whether Blueprint AI can move from philosophy into practical business design.

The first case study is:

```text
Payton Glass Center
```

Payton Glass Center is used to validate the framework, expose missing pieces, and create reusable patterns for future businesses, consulting, teaching, courses, agents, websites, CRM systems, marketing systems, and automation.

---

## 2. Rule of Separation

Do not confuse the first implementation with the entire framework.

Payton Glass Center teaches the framework, but Blueprint AI must remain universal.

When working on the implementation, classify each insight as:

```text
Universal Framework Insight
Payton Glass Center Specific Insight
Template Candidate
Playbook Candidate
Agent Candidate
Automation Candidate
Open Question
```

## 5. Step Three: Offer Map

Document the business offers clearly.

For Payton Glass Center, this should eventually include:

- Group tours plus activity
- Hands-on torch experiences
- Fused glass workshops
- Professional glass art studio identity
- Future deeper classes
- Private events
- Educational visits
- Retail or custom glass opportunities, if applicable

The offer map should identify:

- What the offer is
- Who it is for
- Why they buy it
- How they book it
- What questions they ask
- What proof they need
- What follow-up is required
- How valuable the offer is to the business

---

## 6. Step Four: Customer Journey Map

Map the journey from first discovery to repeat business.

Include:

1. How customers first discover the business
2. What they search for
3. What they need to understand
4. What objections or confusion they have
5. What makes them trust the business
6. How they contact or book
7. What happens before arrival
8. What happens during the experience
9. What happens after the experience
10. How reviews, referrals, and repeat visits are encouraged

---

## 7. Step Five: Search and Discovery Audit

Review all major discovery surfaces.

Include:

- Website
- SEO
- AEO
- GEO
- Google Business Profile
- Bing Places
- Apple Business Connect
- Knowledge graph signals
- Social platforms
- Review platforms
- Structured data opportunities

The goal is to make the business accurately understood by both humans and AI systems.

---

## 8. Step Six: Website and Content Structure

Build a website and content plan from the business structure.

The website should not be treated as a standalone design project. It is the public expression of the business blueprint.

The content system should support:

- Customer education
- Search visibility
- AI answer visibility
- Trust building
- Booking clarity
- Social media reuse
- Teaching material
- Case study evidence

---

## 9. Step Seven: CRM and Follow-Up Design

Map how leads and customers should be handled.

Include:

- Inquiry types
- Contact forms
- Booking requests
- Group tour leads
- Follow-up messages
- Review requests
- Repeat customer prompts
- Email sequences
- Missed opportunity tracking

Do not automate until the process is clear.

---

## 10. Step Eight: AI Agent Opportunities

Identify where AI agents could support the business.

Examples:

- Website content assistant
- Review response assistant
- Customer inquiry assistant
- Social content assistant
- GBP optimization assistant
- CRM follow-up assistant
- Operations documentation assistant
- Business analysis assistant

Each agent idea should include:

- Purpose
- Inputs
- Outputs
- Human approval requirements
- Risk level
- First version scope

---

## 11. Step Nine: Automation Opportunities

Identify where automation can help after the workflow is documented.

Automation candidates may include:

- Lead capture
- Email follow-up
- Review requests
- Booking reminders
- Internal task creation
- Content repurposing
- Reporting dashboards
- Search profile update reminders

---

## 12. Step Ten: Case Study Record

Update:

```text
implementations/payton-glass-center/CASE_STUDY.md
```

Track:

- What was built
- What changed
- What improved
- What failed
- What was learned
- What became reusable
- What should be avoided

---

## 13. Completion Criteria

The first implementation is successful when it produces:

- A clearer business model
- Better documented offers
- Better customer journey understanding
- A stronger public presence plan
- A search and discovery strategy
- A CRM and follow-up plan
- AI and automation opportunities
- Reusable templates and playbooks
- A case study that proves the framework can work

---

## 14. First Implementation Rule

Move fast enough to create momentum, but clearly enough that every step can become reusable.
