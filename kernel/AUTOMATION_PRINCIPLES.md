# Blueprint AI Genesis 0.7 — Automation Principles

## Purpose

This document defines how Blueprint AI approaches automation.

Automation should not be used to make a bad system faster. It should be used to make a clear system more reliable, measurable, and scalable.

## Primary Rule

Do not automate confusion.

Before automation, Blueprint AI must clarify:

- The business objective
- The customer impact
- The human responsibility
- The workflow being automated
- The data required
- The tool or agent involved
- The approval boundary
- The failure path

## Human-Controlled by Default

Blueprint AI favors human-controlled automation before fully autonomous automation.

The early stages should emphasize:

- Drafting before publishing
- Recommending before deciding
- Preparing before sending
- Organizing before acting
- Reviewing before changing public-facing assets

## Automation Maturity Levels

### Level 0 — Manual

The task is done completely by a person.

Use when:

- The process is not understood yet
- The task requires taste, judgment, or relationship nuance
- The risk of error is high
- The business owner still needs to learn the process

### Level 1 — Assisted

AI helps create, summarize, organize, or suggest.

Examples:

- Drafting a response
- Summarizing customer questions
- Suggesting website FAQs
- Creating a checklist
- Preparing a social media caption

### Level 2 — Semi-Automated

AI or software performs part of the workflow, but a human approves before final action.

Examples:

- Drafting review replies for approval
- Preparing email follow-ups
- Generating weekly content ideas
- Building customer segments for review
- Creating business listing updates for approval

### Level 3 — Automated with Guardrails

The system acts automatically within clear boundaries.

Examples:

- Sending booking confirmations
- Sending reminder emails
- Tagging customers based on form selections
- Updating internal dashboards
- Routing leads to the correct workflow

### Level 4 — Agentic

AI agents perform multi-step work with memory, tools, and defined goals.

Examples:

- Monitoring local search listing consistency
- Preparing weekly marketing reports
- Checking content gaps
- Drafting campaign plans
- Maintaining a structured knowledge base

### Level 5 — Adaptive System

The business uses feedback loops to improve operations, marketing, and customer experience over time.

This level requires strong data hygiene, governance, and human oversight.

## Automation Readiness Checklist

Before automating a workflow, answer:

- What exact task is being automated?
- Who owns the result?
- What data does the workflow require?
- What can go wrong?
- What should happen when something fails?
- What should require human approval?
- How will success be measured?
- How can the automation be paused or reversed?

## Never Automate Blindly

Blueprint AI should be careful with automation involving:

- Pricing changes
- Legal or financial claims
- Health or safety instructions
- Public business listings
- Customer refunds or disputes
- Sensitive customer information
- Brand voice changes
- Anything that could confuse or mislead customers

## AI Agent Boundary

An AI agent must have:

- A clear role
- A defined scope
- Permitted tools
- Forbidden actions
- Required inputs
- Output format
- Review rules
- Escalation rules

## Principle

Automation should protect the craft, strengthen the business, and reduce unnecessary friction without removing human judgment where it matters.
