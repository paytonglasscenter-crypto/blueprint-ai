# Blueprint AI Search Visibility Model

**Version:** Genesis 0.6  
**Status:** Foundation  
**Purpose:** Define how Blueprint AI makes a business visible, understandable, trustworthy, and answerable across search engines, AI assistants, maps, business directories, and knowledge systems.

---

## 1. Core Principle

A business cannot be fully AI-powered if it cannot be clearly understood by humans, search engines, AI systems, and local discovery platforms.

Search visibility is not limited to ranking on Google. It includes every surface where a customer, search engine, AI model, map service, directory, or recommendation engine tries to answer:

- What is this business?
- What does it offer?
- Where is it located?
- Who is it for?
- Why should it be trusted?
- What action should someone take next?

Blueprint AI treats visibility as a business architecture layer, not a marketing afterthought.

---

## 2. The Visibility Stack

Blueprint AI organizes search visibility into seven connected layers.

### Layer 1: Business Entity Clarity

The business must be clearly defined as an entity.

This includes:

- Official business name
- Address
- Phone number
- Website
- Categories
- Services
- Products
- Founders or key people
- Location served
- Brand positioning
- Proof of legitimacy

If the entity is unclear, search engines and AI systems will produce weak, inconsistent, or incorrect answers.

### Layer 2: Offer Clarity

The business must clearly explain what customers can buy, book, attend, request, or learn.

Each offer should include:

- Offer name
- Who it is for
- Outcome
- Price or price range when appropriate
- Duration
- Booking method
- Location
- Frequently asked questions
- Supporting proof

### Layer 3: Local Platform Alignment

The business must be consistent across major discovery platforms.

Primary platforms:

- Google Business Profile
- Bing Places
- Apple Business Connect
- Website
- Social platforms
- Major directories
- Review platforms

The website and business profiles should reinforce each other instead of contradicting each other.

### Layer 4: Website Search Structure

The website must contain structured pages that map to the real business.

Core page types:

- Home page
- About page
- Contact page
- Location page
- Offer pages
- Group pages
- FAQ pages
- Gallery pages
- Review or proof pages
- Educational content pages

The website should not be a brochure only. It should function as the canonical knowledge source for the business.

### Layer 5: Answer Engine Optimization

The business must be easy for AI systems to summarize, recommend, and cite.

AEO focuses on direct answers:

- What is the business?
- What services does it offer?
- Is it good for families?
- Is it good for groups?
- What makes it different?
- How do I book?
- What should I expect?

### Layer 6: Generative Engine Optimization

The business must be represented in a way that generative AI systems can use accurately.

GEO focuses on making the business:

- Easy to understand
- Easy to compare
- Easy to recommend
- Easy to include in generated answers
- Supported by clear facts and structured content

### Layer 7: Knowledge Graph Readiness

The business should be structured so machines can understand relationships.

Examples:

- Business → offers → customer segments
- Business → location → service area
- Business → founder → expertise
- Business → reviews → trust proof
- Business → activities → categories
- Business → classes → educational offerings

---

## 3. Visibility Rule

Every public statement about the business should strengthen one or more of these signals:

- Clarity
- Consistency
- Trust
- Relevance
- Authority
- Answerability
- Actionability

If content does not strengthen one of these signals, it should be questioned.

---

## 4. Blueprint AI Visibility Output

For each business implementation, Blueprint AI should eventually create:

- Search entity profile
- Offer map
- Customer journey map
- Local listing audit
- Website page architecture
- FAQ system
- Structured data plan
- Review strategy
- AI answer readiness audit
- Knowledge graph map

---

## 5. Payton Glass Center Validation

Payton Glass Center is the first test case for this model.

Its visibility challenge is not only to rank for glass art terms. It must become clearly understood as:

- A professional glass art studio
- A hands-on activity center
- A group tour destination
- A place for fused glass workshops
- A place for torch-based flameworking experiences
- A local Louisville attraction
- A creative learning destination
- A business with real artists making a living through glass

The website, business listings, search content, and AI answer surfaces must all reinforce those truths.
